AI-Based Route Optimization for Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP)

A comprehensive study and implementation of Genetic Algorithms (GA) and heuristic optimization applied to NP-hard combinatorial path planning across Indian road networks.

SPPU Third Year Computer Engineering Course: Artificial Intelligence Academic Term: 2025-2026

1. Project Aim & Objectives

Aim

To design, build, and deploy an interactive web-based Artificial Intelligence system capable of solving the Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP) by finding near-optimal shortest closed tour sequences using evolutionary Genetic Algorithms and real-world geographical coordinates.

Problem Statement

Given a discrete list of $N$ cities and their geographical coordinates, find the shortest possible closed-loop tour that visits each city exactly once and returns to the initial departure city, minimizing total travel distance and carbon footprint while overcoming the factorial $O(N!)$ time complexity barrier of brute-force search.

Key Objectives
Evolutionary AI Solver Implement pure Python Genetic Algorithm with Tournament Selection, Ordered Crossover (OX1), Swap Mutation, and Elitism.
Dual-Mode Distance Modeling Provide great-circle Haversine calculations and integrate OSRM (Open Source Routing Machine) road-network routing.
Mathematical Verification Incorporate exact brute-force verification for ≤ 9 cities to scientifically benchmark GA heuristic optimality.
Interactive Geospatial Dashboard Interactive Leaflet.js mapping, Chart.js convergence analytics, and SQLite persistence.

2. Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP) & NP-Hard Complexity

The Travelling Salesman Problem is one of the most intensively studied combinatorial optimization problems in computer science and operations research. It belongs to the class of NP-hard problems.

For $N$ cities with a fixed starting origin, the number of possible distinct permutations is given by:

Search Space = \frac{(N - 1)!}{2}   \text{(for undirected graphs)}

This astronomical growth proves why exact brute force is intractable for large networks, necessitating AI metaheuristic algorithms like Genetic Algorithms that converge on high-quality solutions in fractional seconds.

3. Genetic Algorithm Engineering

Chromosome Representation

Each candidate solution is an ordered list of city indices representing the intermediate sequence. The departure hub (index 0) remains permanently anchored at the beginning and end of the closed tour.

Fitness Evaluation

Fitness is inversely proportional to total tour distance: $f(x) = \frac{1}{\text{Distance}(x)}$. Individuals with shorter paths possess higher evolutionary fitness.

Tournament Selection

$k=5$ individuals are randomly chosen from the population, and the fittest among them is selected as a parent. This maintains evolutionary pressure while preserving genetic diversity.

Ordered Crossover (OX1)

Preserves a contiguous sub-tour from Parent 1 and fills the remaining slots from Parent 2 in rotational order without duplicating or omitting any city.

Swap Mutation

With probability $P_m = 0.05$, two random cities in the chromosome exchange positions, introducing stochastic mutations to escape local optima.

Elitism Preservation

The top $E=2$ elite individuals survive directly into the next generation without modification, guaranteeing that the best-found route distance monotonically non-increases.

Genetic Algorithm Workflow
1 Input Cities Load coordinates & distance matrix
2 Initial Pop Random permutations + heuristic seeds
3 Fitness Eval Calculate round-trip km distance
4 Selection Tournament selection (k=5)
5 OX1 Crossover Ordered sub-tour inheritance
6 Swap Mutation Stochastic perturbation (Pm=0.05)
7 Elitism Preserve best 2 chromosomes
8 Termination Generations reached → Best route

5. Practical Applications & Analysis

Applications
  • E-Commerce last-mile parcel delivery routing
  • Public transit & school bus fleet scheduling
  • PCB drilling & CNC machining head path optimization
  • Emergency medical dispatch & aid distribution
Advantages
  • Rapid convergence compared to exhaustive search
  • Escapes local minima via stochastic mutation
  • Scalable to large numbers of waypoints
  • Supports arbitrary distance matrices (geodesic or road)
Limitations
  • Stochastic metaheuristic: yields near-optimal, not guaranteed global optimum for large N
  • Dependent on parameter tuning ($P_m$, generations, pop size)
  • External road APIs subject to network availability & rate limits

Conclusion

This project demonstrates how nature-inspired evolutionary Artificial Intelligence effectively solves complex combinatorial optimization challenges. By combining Genetic Algorithms with real-world spatial APIs and a modern responsive user experience, the system provides practical, measurable travel distance and carbon reductions.
